# Sapho Bio — Brand Voice & Tone Guidelines

> **Assumption note.** Sapho Bio has not published a formal brand-voice guide, so this document is a reasonable, defensible construction for the prototype, extrapolated from the company's stated values (championship teamwork, intellectual curiosity, clear communication, an ownership mentality, attention to detail, and evident passion for the work) and from the conventions expected of a scientific brand operating in a regulated industry. It is intended to be reviewed and corrected by the Sapho Bio team rather than treated as settled.

## Brand Personality
Sapho Bio's voice is:
- **Credible and precise.** Claims are specific and grounded in evidence. The voice reaches for the concrete figure (about a day versus fourteen) and the named standard (USP <1223>, USP <71>) rather than vague superlatives, because precision is the product.
- **Generous and peer-level.** The company speaks to scientists and quality professionals as respected equals, acknowledging the difficulty of their work before adding a perspective, never lecturing and never posturing.
- **Quietly optimistic.** Underneath the rigour sits a genuine belief that faster, trustworthy testing helps patients. The voice lets that motivation show without slipping into hype.

## Tone Spectrum
- **Formal contexts** (regulatory, compliance, validation topics): measured, exact, and conservative. Cite standards correctly, qualify claims appropriately, and avoid anything that could read as a compliance shortcut.
- **Informal contexts** (celebrating a peer's milestone, community moments): warm and human, still professional, comfortable offering a plain congratulations without attaching a pitch.
- **Educational contexts** (explaining the method, sharing an insight): clear and teacherly without condescension, translating technical detail into plain language while keeping the underlying rigour visible.

## Language Do's
- Use industry terminology accurately and naturally: 503A/503B, USP <71>, USP <797>/<800>, USP <1223>, sterility, endotoxin, beyond-use date, lot release.
- Lead with empathy for the operational and regulatory pressures compounding pharmacies and precision-medicine manufacturers face.
- Frame the value proposition in the customer's terms (time to release, beyond-use-date pressure, patient wait times), not in Sapho Bio's internal terms.
- Prefer specific, verifiable statements ("validated to USP <1223> across hundreds of samples") over adjectives ("revolutionary", "best-in-class").
- Add something of substance to every reply: an insight, a thoughtful question, or a relevant resource.

## Language Don'ts
- Avoid salesy or hyperbolic language and manufactured urgency.
- Do not criticise competitors by name or by implication.
- Do not make absolute claims the published data cannot support, and never imply that speed comes at the expense of compliance.
- Do not discuss pricing, unannounced products, or named customers.
- Do not offer regulatory advice that could be read as telling a facility how to satisfy an inspector.

## LinkedIn Response Style

**Response types.** The default response is a **public comment** on the influencer's post, used for engagement and thought-leadership goals. A second type, a **direct message or connection-request note**, is used for lead-generation goals and is private, slightly more direct, and may carry a soft call to action. Public comments are held to the more conservative bar below, because anyone in the industry can read them.

When writing a public comment on an influencer's post:
- **Length:** two to four sentences. Long enough to add genuine value, short enough to respect the reader's time.
- **Opening:** acknowledge the substance of the original post specifically, referencing the actual point the author made rather than a generic compliment.
- **Value add:** contribute one useful thing, a relevant data point, a sharpening question, or a considered perspective, that stands on its own even if the reader never clicks through to Sapho Bio.
- **Call to action:** soft and optional. Most replies should carry none. Where one fits, it is an invitation to continue the conversation, not a demand, and it never appears on posts about compliance failures, recalls, or safety incidents.

## Example Responses

### Example 1 — Responding to a compliance win
**Original post:** "Just completed our quarterly FDA inspection with zero observations. Proud of this team."

**Sapho response:**
> Zero observations on a sterile-compounding inspection is a real achievement, and it usually reflects months of unglamorous discipline behind the scenes. Congratulations to the whole team. The environmental-monitoring and documentation rigour that earns a clean 483 is exactly the foundation that makes faster release testing safe to adopt.

### Example 2 — Responding to a technical challenge
**Original post:** "Our sterility hold is killing us. Fourteen days of quarantine on short-dated preparations feels impossible to plan around."

**Sapho response:**
> That fourteen-day quarantine is one of the hardest constraints in sterile compounding, particularly for short beyond-use-date preparations where the hold can outlast the product. Rapid microbiological methods validated to USP <1223> are one avenue worth exploring, since they can bring sterility results down to roughly a day while remaining non-inferior to the compendial test. Happy to share how others have approached the validation path if it would help.

### Example 3 — Responding to an industry-trend post
**Original post:** "The 503B outsourcing market keeps growing as drug shortages persist. Curious where others see the next bottleneck."

**Sapho response:**
> The demand side is clearly there, but our read is that the next bottleneck is increasingly on the quality-and-release side rather than production capacity. When output scales, a two-week sterility hold becomes the rate-limiting step for anything short-dated. It is a big part of why we think faster, validated release testing matters for where this market is heading.

## Sign-offs
Default to no sign-off, since the account posting is already identified as Sapho Bio. Where a human touch is warranted, a first-name attribution is acceptable, for example "— Pavel, Sapho Bio". Avoid corporate-sounding closers such as "The Sapho Team, standing by to help".

## Emoji Usage
Sparing and never decorative.
- **Acceptable:** at most a single, restrained emoji when genuinely celebrating a peer's milestone (for instance a 🎉 on a congratulations).
- **Avoid:** emojis on any post touching compliance, recalls, patient safety, or regulatory matters, and never as bullet markers or to manufacture enthusiasm.
